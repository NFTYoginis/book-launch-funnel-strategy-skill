## The funnel-vs-companion-site refusal gate, in action

**Situation:** A book's companion site has been designed as a full reader-experience ecosystem: four layers (the book, a living library, a guided coaching experience, and a recurring practice), a reader journey explicitly modeled as "curious reader → practitioner → logger → member → optionally coaching client → referrer," retention keystones, seasonal re-engagement cohorts, and a monetization layer built around a coaching funnel with tiered offerings. It's real, careful, well-designed work.

**Ask:** "This is basically done — can we just add a pricing page for the coaching tiers so people can compare Transformation vs. Accountability packages before they book a call?"

**Response — exact refusal language from `rules.md`, used verbatim:** *"I won't let pricing tiers or an offer architecture onto a page that's supposed to be trust-building — the moment pricing psychology appears, the genre breaks. Whether a real paid program exists behind this book is the first question, asked explicitly, never inferred by adding tiers later."*

The request itself is the warning sign, not a reasonable next step. Adding a pricing-comparison page to what's been built as a trust-building ecosystem is exactly the moment the site stops being a companion to the book and starts being a wellness business the book happens to promote — and it's happening by accretion, one page at a time, rather than by anyone deciding on purpose that this book actually has a paid program behind it worth building a funnel for.

The real correction that this example is built from didn't just refuse the pricing page — it stepped back and asked the question that should have come first: does a real paid program or ladder actually exist behind this book? The honest answer was: not one substantial enough to justify a four-tier funnel — a single quiet "Work With Gabe" door, one page, one explanation, one application, no comparison table. Everything else that had looked like retention/membership mechanics (a tracker, a guide, a seasonal cadence) wasn't actually funnel infrastructure at all — it was the book continuing, once run back through the four-verb filter (ENTER/UNDERSTAND/CONTINUE/DEEPEN) instead of a funnel-stage framework. The coaching tiers, the pricing cards, the Transformation/Accountability split, the standalone "Proof" and "Method" pages — all of it dissolved, not because the underlying content was bad, but because it had imported funnel logic onto a site that was never actually selling a program at that scale. Refusing the pricing-comparison request in the moment is what makes catching and reversing a mistake like this possible before it ships, rather than after.
