# Channel sequencing — owned → borrowed → earned

The order launch outreach actually runs in, and why the order matters as much as the list itself. Read directly from `GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/launch/author-pitch-list.md`, a real, complete channel-sequencing plan.

## Why order matters, not just coverage

The real trail's own sequencing rationale: *"Owned first (this week) — your list, your podcast, your socials. Prove the assets convert before spending goodwill on cold pitches."* A cold pitch to a podcast or a publication is a one-shot use of goodwill — if the outlet says yes, there's no do-over if the assets behind it (the book's own page, the excerpt, the pitch) turn out weak. Proving conversion on owned channels first means every earned-channel pitch that follows is backed by evidence the material actually works, not hope that it will.

## The three tiers

1. **Owned — do these first, this week.** Zero gatekeepers, fastest signal. The author's own email list (the launch sequence — see `reference/email-sequence-structure.md`), their own podcast or platform, their own social channels, the companion site itself. The real trail's own framing: *"The launch lives or dies here before any cold pitch matters."*
2. **Borrowed — next, warm and low-friction.** Existing relationships and adjacent audiences: cross-promotion to readers of the author's other work, the author's own professional network, warm community relationships, any program or audience the author already has standing in. Lower effort than earned, because trust already exists; still requires genuine fit, not just proximity.
3. **Earned — last, cold outreach, tiered by fit.** Podcasts, publications, newsletters the author has no existing relationship with. Tier by genuine fit (does the outlet's actual audience and stance match the book's thesis) before tiering by reach — a smaller outlet that's a strong fit outperforms a larger one that isn't. **One customized asset per outlet, never a blast pitch.**

## Qualifying targets honestly before pitching

The real trail's own guardrail, worth preserving exactly: earned-channel targets are **"targets to qualify, not secured placements."** Before any pitch goes out: confirm the outlet still exists, still runs this kind of piece, and what its current submission path actually is. **No fabricated contacts** — name outlets and angles, not invented editor relationships. This keeps a channel-sequencing plan honest as a plan, not a list of assumed yeses.

## Tiering earned targets by fit, not just reach

Within the earned tier, separate targets into fit-tiers (the real trail used A/B) and flag known tensions honestly rather than pretending every target is a clean fit. The real trail's own example: an outlet whose audience or hosts sell exactly the kind of hype the book debunks is still a real option — the honest-debunk angle can land there — but it goes in "eyes open," flagged for the tension rather than treated as an uncomplicated match.

## The reusable cold-pitch structure

One customized email per target, built from a fixed skeleton with outlet-specific brackets filled in: a debunk-forward subject line, a one-line reason this outlet/now (the peg), a brief honest-authority bio (no fabricated credentials or outcome stats), the specific ask (an op-ed, a guest excerpt, a podcast slot), the differentiator stated plainly (what makes this pitch different from the usual version of the same pitch), and a single link. The skeleton stays fixed; only the bracketed specifics change per outlet — this is what keeps "one customized asset per outlet" achievable at real volume rather than a nice idea that collapses into a blast pitch under time pressure.

## Worked reference — the real sequencing, in order

1. Owned, this week: the author's list runs the email sequence; the author's own podcast runs a solo episode on the book's thesis; social channels carry the excerpt hooks and signature cards; the companion site runs the excerpt drops as blog content.
2. Borrowed, next: cross-promotion to the readers of the author's other books (the warmest list there is, same author, adjacent audience); the author's own professional network; an existing warm community relationship.
3. Earned, last: Tier-A podcasts and trade publications that are a strong, obvious fit go first; Tier-B (bigger reach, more of a stretch, sometimes a real tension to flag) go after; op-eds pegged to a relevant seasonal moment; newsletters and Substacks last, confirmed active before pitching.
