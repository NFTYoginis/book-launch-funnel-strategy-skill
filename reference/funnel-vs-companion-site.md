# Funnel vs. companion site — the core judgment call

The single most valuable piece of evidence this skill is built from, because it's a documented mistake and its own fix, not a clean success story. Read directly from `GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/BOOK-WEBSITE-MAP.md`, the real correction document, and `GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/design/ECOSYSTEM-ARCHITECTURE.md`, the real "before" state it replaced.

## What happened, in order

1. **A full ecosystem got designed first.** Four layers (Philosophy/book, Living Library/website, Guided Experience/coaching, The Practice/rituals), a reader journey explicitly modeled as "curious reader → practitioner → logger → member → optionally coaching client → referrer," retention keystones, seasonal re-engagement cohorts, and a monetization layer built around a coaching funnel. This is real, sophisticated work — not a naive mistake.
2. **It was recognized as miscast.** The site wasn't a wellness business with a book attached to it — it was a book with a companion site. The ecosystem architecture had quietly imported funnel logic (membership tiers, an offer ladder, retention mechanics borrowed from SaaS/coaching businesses) onto something that was actually a publishing platform.
3. **It was corrected**, not patched. `BOOK-WEBSITE-MAP.md` explicitly retires the prior strategy docs ("V1–V3... detox → fasting → funnel") and re-derives the site from a single filter.

## The filter, verbatim

> Does this help the reader **ENTER · UNDERSTAND · CONTINUE · or DEEPEN** the book?

Every proposed surface, page, or feature runs through this filter. Four verbs, each with a job:
- **ENTER** — the on-ramp. The question the book asks, in its most inviting form.
- **UNDERSTAND** — the answer, and the proof. Why the book is worth the reader's time.
- **CONTINUE** — the book's own material, extended. What was flagged in a chapter, followed through on the site.
- **DEEPEN** — going further, for the reader who wants more. Still in service of the book, not a separate destination.

An element that doesn't map to one of these four doesn't belong — full stop, regardless of how much work went into building it.

## What survived the filter, and what dissolved

**Survived:** a home page (ENTER), the book's own sales/info page (UNDERSTAND), an assessment/question tool that captures an email while staying ask-don't-score (ENTER), the Living Library as the book's continuing appendix (CONTINUE/DEEPEN), an About page (UNDERSTAND, trust not sell).

**Survived, but changed shape:** the evidence tiers (the STRONG/MODERATE/SPECULATIVE research, real IP and a genuine trust asset) moved from having their own "Proof" menu item into living inside the Library as the book's honesty made visible — same content, no longer treated as a marketing trophy case.

**Dissolved:** a "Method" page (its content redistributed into the book's own pages rather than getting its own kingdom). A "Podcast" page and its associated signup flow (deleted entirely — there was no actual podcast). Most importantly: **Coaching and Programs collapsed into a single "Work With Gabe" door** — one page, one explanation, one application, explicitly **no pricing tiers, no Transformation/Accountability split, no offer architecture.**

## The reframe that matters most

The real trail's own insight, worth encoding precisely: elements that looked like "retention hooks" and "membership on-ramps" in the ecosystem framing — a tracker, a guide, a seasonal cadence — were **not actually funnel mechanics**. They were the book *continuing*. A tracker that lets a reader log their own patterns over years isn't a retention hook bolted onto a business; it's the book still doing its job, later. Reframing the same features from "funnel mechanics" to "the book, alive" didn't require removing them — it required recognizing what they already were.

## The warning sign, named explicitly

Quoted directly, because it's the single most useful diagnostic this skill has: **"The moment pricing psychology appears, the genre breaks."** A trust-building page — an About page, a Work-With-Gabe page, a Living Library resource — that starts to carry tiers, a "compare plans" table, or an offer ladder has stopped being what it claimed to be. This is checkable, not a matter of taste: grep for pricing language, tier names, or comparison tables on any page that's supposed to be building trust rather than closing a sale. If it's there, the page has drifted into funnel territory even if nobody decided to put it there on purpose.

## How to apply this to a new book

Ask the diagnostic question first, explicitly, before designing anything: **does a real paid program or ladder actually exist behind this book?**

- **If yes** — the funnel model applies. Design the ladder deliberately, with real tiers and real pricing, and don't pretend it's something else. A funnel built honestly, as a funnel, is not the mistake this file documents.
- **If no** — the companion-site model applies. Every page runs through the four-verb filter. No page gets pricing tiers or an offer architecture, even a single "quiet door" page pointing at future work should stay to one explanation, one application — not a menu.

The mistake this file documents wasn't building an ecosystem — it was building funnel mechanics onto a book that didn't have a paid program behind it, without ever asking the question explicitly first. Asking first is the whole fix.
